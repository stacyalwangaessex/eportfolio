Team exercises: individual contributions

Development Team Project: Pampered Pets risk identification report (Group 2)

Artefact: Pampered_Pets_Risk_Identification_Report_2.docx (original submission) · Full text

What the task was. The Development Team Project asked Group 2 to act as a security consultancy responding to the Pampered Pets scenario: a four-person micro-enterprise (four staff, two networked computers, a single shared wireless gateway, ~90% of trade face to face) facing an investor's proposal to digitalise (e-commerce storefront, cloud inventory, email marketing) and to move to international sourcing. The team produced a risk identification report assessing both the status quo and the proposed transformation, and recommending a proportionate course of action.

What the report contains.

  • Methodology: ISO/IEC 27005:2022 adopted, justified against rejected alternatives (NIST SP 800-30 oriented to US federal systems; OCTAVE Allegro presumes internal analytical capacity a four-person firm lacks), with a qualitative five-point likelihood/impact matrix; Alice identified as risk owner with low appetite for regulatory and moderate appetite for commercial risk.
  • Assessment 1, the status quo: six risks (R1-R6) covering phishing/business email compromise, ransomware against the unsupported warehouse machine, lateral movement from personal devices, irrecoverable data loss, UK GDPR accountability, and customer migration to online competitors, with mitigations (verified cloud backup, network segregation, MFA, acceptable-use and incident-response policy, ICO registration, Cyber Essentials benchmarking).
  • Assessment 2, the digitalisation programme: six risks (D1-D6) covering web skimming/formjacking of the checkout, payment fraud and PCI DSS validation, wider personal data processing and overseas transfers, supply-chain compromise, loss of local provenance and the ten-minute contingency, and the capability gap of four staff absorbing new systems, with mitigations (hosted payment page and SAQ A, CSP and Subresource Integrity, DPIA before launch, dual-sourcing of suppliers, managed security provider and cyber insurance).
  • Recommendations: proceed with digitalisation incrementally, with the Phase 1 security baseline established before any customer-facing system goes live; none of the three figures put to the consultancy (50% revenue uplift, 24% cost saving, 33% attrition) accepted as stated. Includes an indicative twelve-month phased implementation timeline.
  • Scale: 898 words main body (excluding title block, risk registers, figure and reference list), Cite Them Right Harvard referencing.

My individual contribution. Within Group 2, my individual contribution centred on the risk evaluation stage of the report: assisting in the scoring of the identified risks against the five-point likelihood/impact matrix in both assessments, the status quo (R1-R6) and the digitalisation programme (D1-D6), and in the evaluation judgements that fed the team's recommendation to proceed only with a phased, baseline-first digitalisation. The team reached its decisions by consensus rather than disagreement; in particular, the group jointly agreed that none of the investor's three headline figures (50% revenue uplift, 24% cost saving, 33% attrition) should be accepted as stated.

Supporting evidence. No separate documentary evidence is held for this statement; it rests on the submitted report itself and the consistency of the account above with the report's methodology and registers.
