**Peer Response**

Kaona, I agree with your account, particularly the accountability gap you identify: an investigation can appear rigorous precisely because it is data-driven, while concealing uncertain assumptions and the human decisions behind its conclusions. Your point that data collected for protection can expose vulnerable people to surveillance and retaliation is also well made, and I would add that this risk is predictable enough to be designed against rather than merely managed after the fact.

Where I would build on your argument is in weighting the mitigations. Data-sharing agreements, data minimisation and retention rules are all necessary, but they only hold if the communities concerned hold real decision-making power, as you argue. Katell et al. (2020) show that participatory design surfaces harms that occur even when a system operates exactly as intended, which supports your distinction between consultation and shared authority: participation without the power to veto or amend a design risks legitimising decisions that have already been made. Hancock et al. (2024) reach the same conclusion from the maritime context, favouring deeper, localised engagement over top-down monitoring.

A question back to you: at what project stage should a community hold the power to say no to a technology altogether?
